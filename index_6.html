Dr. Patryk Krakowiak Neurovirology  Immunology

Herpes simplex virus 1 (HSV‑1) is a pervasive human pathogen that infects over 67% of the world’s population.

HSV‑1 establishes lifelong latency in neurons, primarily in the peripheral nervous system (PNS). However, increasing evidence also suggests that HSV‑1 can persist asymptomatic in the brain, and viral DNA can be detected in the central nervous system (CNS) with increased frequency of occurrence with age.

My studies focus on molecular mechanism of HSV‑1 reactivation from latent infection and how it co‑opts immune pathways in neurons.

Research

Latency. Stress.
Reactivation.

After the first infection, HSV‑1 never leaves. It stays silent inside neurons for life. His work explains what happens inside the cell at the moment the virus decides to return.

Phase 0 · Latency
Phase 0

Latency.

The viral genome sits silenced inside the neuron’s nucleus. No replication, and nothing for the immune system to see. It can stay this way for decades.

Phase I

Stress signal.

Neuronal stress switches on pathways such as c‑Jun. UL12.5 makes mitochondria release nucleic acids, and the cell’s innate alarm goes off.

Phase II

Reactivation.

Instead of stopping the virus, the alarm helps it wake. Without UL12.5, reactivation fails. Switching the same pathways on directly restores it. New virions travel down the axon.
